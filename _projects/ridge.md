## Overview

RIDGE connects remote-sensing data processing with real-time environments. The framework combines satellite imagery, digital elevation models, vegetation masks, building detections, and OpenStreetMap roads into terrain and object assets for Unity and X-Plane.

We developed RIDGE as a four-student graduation project at Başkent University, supervised by **Assoc. Prof. Emre Sümer**, with support from the **HAVELSAN SUIT program** and satellite data provided by **IMPROSAT**.

## My contribution

My main responsibilities were **vegetation detection and the Unity pipeline architecture**. I worked on the Python preprocessing and Unity C# integration that connect spatial data to generated terrain and vegetation.

- Processed NDVI data to identify vegetation regions and produce masks.
- Integrated masks into Unity for procedural vegetation placement.
- Worked on HCV-based color analysis for terrain material classification.
- Designed the Unity-side pipeline architecture and its connection to the preprocessing workflow.

Building detection, road generation, and other parts of the system were collaborative team contributions. The X-Plane branch is a separate simulator export workflow; my primary implementation responsibility was the Unity pipeline.

## A shared spatial workflow

1. Align satellite imagery and elevation data while retaining geographic metadata.
2. Prepare vegetation, building, and road representations.
3. Generate terrain and reusable scene assets for the target environment.
4. Export using the Unity and X-Plane workflows, with shared tile indexing and spatial references.

## Manuscript

**An automated spatial-data integration framework for satellite imagery, elevation data, and simulator-ready 3D terrain**

**Pelinsu Kaleli**, Bulut Demir, Alper Keskin, Zeynep Sude İmdat, and Emre Sümer.

Submitted to the Journal of Spatial Science.

The manuscript reports a building intersection-over-union score of **0.6564** with XUNet and vegetation intersection-over-union of **0.5189**, evaluated against manually digitized reference polygons in the study area. These are study-specific measurements, rather than a general accuracy claim for arbitrary imagery.
